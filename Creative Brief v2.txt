Cellular Automata Granular Audio Engine - Creative Brief (V2)
Project Overview

Version 2 of this project is an evolution of the original Cellular Automata granular audio instrument. The fundamental philosophy remains unchanged:

The audio is NOT controlled by the Cellular Automata. The audio IS the Cellular Automata.

Version 1 sonified cell state faithfully but failed to preserve one of the most beautiful aspects of Cellular Automata: the emergence of spatial structures.

Version 2 introduces Topological Sonification with a revised axiom:

Patterns and coherent structures are the fundamental musical objects of the instrument.

The Cellular Automata is not a collection of interesting pixels that happen to form patterns. The patterns themselves are what the audio engine must listen to.

The goal is to sonify:

Structures and pattern formation
Topology and geometry
Pattern movement
Pattern evolution
Spatial relationships between structures
Colour as regional material
Global energy distribution

The geometry of the simulation should become audible.

Core Design Philosophy

We are NOT building:

A granular synthesiser controlled by visuals
An audiovisual music visualiser
A sonification of individual pixel colours
A collection of DSP effects mapped to Cellular Automata
An instrument with an aesthetic preference for wash, stability, or chaos

We ARE building:

One Cellular Automata universe
One shared mathematical state
Two renderers:
Visual Renderer
Audio Renderer

Both renderers observe the same underlying computational universe.

Every sonic event must be directly derived from properties that already exist within the Cellular Automata simulation.

No arbitrary audio systems should be introduced unless they correspond to a fundamental property of the simulation.

The audio engine must not impose beauty. Its responsibility is to faithfully reveal the behaviour of the simulation. The simulation itself determines geometry, behaviour, colour, movement, temporal evolution, and energy distribution.

Visual Engine

The visual simulation continues to use the existing Utomata Cellular Automata engine.

The Cellular Automata provides:

Cell positions
RGB values
Neighbour relationships
Temporal evolution
Global simulation state
Editable CA equations
A continuous toroidal spatial topology

The audio engine observes these properties in real time and groups them into coherent structures.

Fundamental Musical Objects

V2 prioritises listening to structures, not cells.

A structure is a coherent region derived from neighbour relationships already present in the simulation (lit cells grown by local colour similarity). Structures are not invented by computer-vision classifiers; they are explicit readings of local CA relationships.

Large structures should sound large.
Small structures should sound small.

The audio engine must represent the geometry of the simulation rather than collapsing coherent regions into single listening points.

AUDIO ARCHITECTURE

The audio engine is divided into five layers.

Layer 1 - Spatial Topology

Structure probe positions on the X and Y axes are fundamental audio parameters.

X Axis — Sample Position

The horizontal position of a structure probe determines where within the source audio the grain is extracted.

Left side of the simulation = beginning of the source audio.
Right side of the simulation = end of the source audio.

Horizontal movement of structures becomes movement through the source material.

Y Axis — Frequency / Spectral Position

The vertical position of a structure probe determines where within the frequency spectrum the grain exists.

Bottom of the simulation = lower frequencies.
Top of the simulation = higher frequencies.

Vertical movement of structures becomes spectral movement.

Topology Continuity

If a structure moves across the simulation, the listener must be able to follow that movement.

Stable structures must not freeze sonically simply because they use longer grains.

Voice identity, voice persistence, and X/Y updates must preserve continuous topological movement. The listener should hear structures travelling through the simulation, not a sequence of disconnected grain triggers.

Large structures receive multiple probes along their extent so their geometry remains audible.

Layer 2 - Colour as Regional Material

Colour is an emergent property of neighbouring cells and must be treated as an emergent property of coloured regions and structures.

The visual system does not perceive individual pixel colours in isolation. It perceives coherent colour regions and gradients. The audio system must behave similarly.

Colour must ONLY influence the temporal material properties of grains emitted by a coloured structure or region.

Examples of allowed parameters:

Attack characteristics
Release characteristics
Window curvature
Temporal energy distribution within the grain

Colour must NOT affect:

Sample position
Frequency position
Spectral brightness
Spectral filtering
Voice allocation
Retrigger behaviour
Global gain

Colour must not be treated as three independent audio parameters, a spectral processor, additional filtering, a collection of DSP effects, or a per-grain timbral engine.

The resulting material changes should be subtle and musical. The listener should perceive that structures have different material characteristics rather than hearing obvious DSP processing.

Layer 3 - Spatial Relationships

Spatial relationships are derived from structure geometry and relationships between structures.

These determine:

Voice mass (large structures claim more sonic presence)
Probe layout along structure extent
Grain length and overlap from internal coherence and differentiation within a structure

Voice allocation must provide a representative sonic snapshot of the simulation. It must not prioritise ambient wash, stability, or chaos as aesthetic goals.

Large stable structures sound beautiful because they exist within the simulation, not because the audio engine is biased towards them. Chaotic behaviour may emerge naturally without becoming overwhelming or underrepresented.

Layer 4 - Temporal Relationships

Temporal relationships are derived from structure persistence, velocity, and evolution.

These determine:

Grain length
Overlap
Motion-driven topology refresh
Voice persistence and identity continuity

Rapidly evolving or moving structures must refresh their topological (X/Y) sounding locus so movement remains audible. Temporally stable structures may sustain without unnecessary retrigger chatter, while still updating topology when they translate.

Layer 5 - Global Relationships

Global simulation properties determine:

Master Gain
Maximum Simultaneous Voices
Energy Distribution

Potential measurements include:

Total structure mass
Total presence / information across structures
Number of active structures

The audio engine should normalise itself against the total energy of the simulation so the universe contains a finite amount of sonic energy that is continuously redistributed as structures emerge and evolve.

Toroidal Topology

The Cellular Automata exists within a continuous toroidal space.

If structures wrap around the simulation boundaries, they should remain sonically continuous wherever possible. The audio engine must preserve topological continuity rather than introducing artificial discontinuities at the seams.

Source Audio

The user should be able to load:

WAV
MP3
AIFF

The source audio continues to function as the raw material from which all grains are derived.

The Cellular Automata simulation continuously explores and redistributes this material across:

Sample position (structure X)
Frequency position (structure Y)
Regional grain material (structure colour envelopes)
Spatial relationships between structures
Temporal evolution of structures

Final Creative Goal

The goal of Version 2 is to create an audiovisual instrument that allows users to listen to the topology of emergence.

The listener should be able to hear:

The geometry of computational structures.
The movement of those structures through sample and frequency space.
The material character of coloured regions.
The redistribution of sonic energy as patterns form and collapse.
The equal validity of stable and chaotic regimes as determined by the simulation.

The beauty of the visuals emerges from simple local relationships between cells.

The beauty of the sound emerges from those exact same relationships, heard as structures rather than as isolated cells.

Version 2 is not intended to sound like granular synthesis processed with effects. It should feel like listening to the geometry and material of a living computational universe as it evolves in real time.
