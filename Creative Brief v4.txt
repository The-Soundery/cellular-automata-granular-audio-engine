------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Project Overview
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This project is a browser-based audiovisual instrument that lets a listener hear
a Cellular Automata simulation as a finite sonic universe.

Core axiom:

  The audio must NOT be controlled by the Cellular Automata.
  The audio IS the Cellular Automata.

Visual output and audio output are two sensory renderings of the same underlying
mathematical system. Granular synthesis is the chosen listening medium — not a
synthesizer that the CA modulates.

What you are listening to:

  The movement and evolution of computational information through time —
  emergence, propagation, interaction, and transformation — heard as sonic
  events / grains that emerge from that activity.

Cellular Automata are not interesting because of the static state of individual
cells. They are interesting because of continuous computational flow. The sound
must evolve as the visuals evolve.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Core Design Philosophy
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We are NOT building:

- A granular synthesizer intelligently playing the Cellular Automata
- An audiovisual music visualiser
- A composer, curator, or interestingness engine
- Explicit structure detection that asks which structure deserves a voice
- Aesthetic preference for wash, stability, chaos, beauty, or intensity
- Arbitrary DSP mappings disconnected from CA mathematics
- Voice ownership, structure prioritisation, interestingness scores,
  stable-vs-chaotic loudness pools, or sustain-vs-active voice systems
- Fixed listening points, persistent sonic locations, or lattice “ears”
- Luminance → volume as a substitute for hearing computational flow

We ARE building:

- One Cellular Automata universe
- One shared mathematical state
- Two renderings: visual and audio
- A physically constrained audio renderer of computational evolution
- An instrument whose sonic complexity emerges from CA behaviour itself
- Observational regions (coherence / chaos) as field measurements that set
  grain laws — never as owned voices or curated priorities


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sonic Laws (authoritative)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One-sentence axiom:

  The CA field is observed as coherence regions and chaotic areas; each area’s
  share of a finite grain pool is proportional to its size; within that share,
  change and coherence set rate, length, and overlap; grain identity at spawn
  is hue→sample, X→pan, Y→spectrum; sample window freezes at spawn while
  region-bound grains directly follow pan/Y with their region; loudness is
  globally neutral.


1. Grains are ephemeral carriers of identity

   A grain is a short-lived sonic event. When it spawns it carries identity from
   the field at that moment. It does not own a place in the grid. The next grain
   may carry different identity. There are no persistent sonic posts.


2. Grain identity (what the grain is made of)

   From the spawn cell (or calm spawn site):

   - Hue(RGB) → sample position / locked scrub window in the source
                (similar colours scrub similar sample areas) — FROZEN at spawn
   - X        → stereo pan (−1 left … +1 right) — initial at spawn; while a
                grain belongs to a region it directly follows that region’s
                motion in pan (spawn offset from COM preserved)
   - Y        → frequency / spectral position — same direct region follow
   - Envelope → fixed material shape at spawn (not driven by RGB)

   Colour chooses where in the source the grain listens. Spatial X places the
   grain in the stereo field. Colour must never set loudness, allocation, or
   topology. Luminance is not volume.


3. Field observation (how we see regimes)

   Every CA step, with spatial and temporal averaging so regimes are stable
   enough to hear (not micro-flicker):

   - δ   local rate of change (smoothed colour/state delta)
   - s   local colour similarity (inverse of neighbour variance)
   - κ   coherence = similarity × stability (high only when BOTH hold)
   - ℓ   coherence length / extent (how far similarity reaches)

   Group into observational regions:

   - Coherent region: similar colour, slow changing, with measurable area A,
     centre of mass (x̄, ȳ), mean change δ̄, mean coherence κ̄, bounding
     width/height, and COM velocity from smoothed COM motion.
   - Chaotic area: low κ / high δ — treated as a bag of cells (no meaningful
     centre-follow requirement).

   Regions are re-derived each frame with smoothing. They are measurements,
   not voice owners. Do not score “interestingness” or prefer one regime.


4. Region laws → grain function (how grains behave)

   Spawn rate     ∝ region mean change δ̄
                  (faster change → more frequent fires)

   Duration       ∝ κ̄ and grows with area A
                  (large calm areas → long grains)

   Overlap        ∝ κ̄ × size
                  (calm → overlap allowed; chaos → overlap ≈ 0)

   Calm packing:  few long overlapping grains → one continuous texture
   Chaos packing: many short non-overlapping grains from random cells
                  inside the chaotic area

   The surrounding relationships determine the laws. The grain spawn location
   supplies hue, X, and Y. The trigger and behaviour come from region
   observation.


5. Freeze at spawn (anti-glitch law) — revised

   Frozen at birth (must not chase):

   - Sample / scrub window and ping-pong bounds
   - Playback direction
   - Envelope shape (attack/release fractions for that grain’s life)

   Allowed to track directly after birth (any grain with a region id):

   - Stereo pan ← region COM X + spawn offset (equal-power, no smoothing)
   - Spectral Y ← region COM Y + spawn offset (no smoothing)

   Forbidden: modulating the sample scrub window during playback to chase a
   moving region or cell (causes undesirable glitches / pitch tears).

   How region texture follows motion: grains that belong to a coherent region
   move their pan and spectrum with that region; sample material stays locked;
   new grains also spawn from the updated mask/colour; overlap crossfades
   create continuous evolution. Chaos-bag grains have no region id and keep
   spawn pan/Y.


6. Geometry and colour laws at spawn (setup for each grain)

   Decided only at spawn:

   - Hue → sample window + ping-pong: spawn-cell hue maps to a sample range
     locked at spawn (calm wider than chaos). If the read head reaches either
     edge before the grain ends, it reverses and continues (ping-pong) until
     the grain’s defined length completes.
   - X → pan: spawn cell horizontal position maps to equal-power stereo pan.
   - Motion → direction: if the region’s COM is moving left/right, playback
     direction may be forward or reverse to match that motion.
   - Height → Y spread: tall coherent regions bias the few calm grains toward
     varied Y spawn heights (fuller harmonic spread). Thin/short regions keep
     a narrower Y spread.

   These are physics of colour, geometry, and motion — not aesthetic effects.


7. Finite grain budget (area spends capacity; activity does not buy priority)

   There is a maximum concurrent grain count (physical constraint).

   Each region’s share of that budget ∝ A / total area.
   Large stable areas that dominate the simulation receive most of the budget
   and spend it as few long overlapping grains. Chaotic areas receive share
   by their area and spend it as many short hits.

   Activity (δ̄) shapes rate, length, and overlap — how the share is used.
   Activity must NOT increase loudness or sonic priority.


8. Volume is a finite neutral resource

   Master / total sonic energy stays roughly constant whether the simulation
   is all calm, all chaotic, or mixed.

   Neither chaotic nor stable regions may be louder or quieter as a policy.
   Their balance in the output follows their weighting in the simulation
   (area share → grain-budget share → texture character).
   What changes is grain usage and texture, not mix-desk preference.

   Amplitude normalisation across active grains enforces neutrality.
   More grains or faster firing must not equal a louder mix.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Audio Engine as Constrained Renderer
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The audio engine does not decide what deserves to be heard, and does not ask
what a fixed point in space should sound like.

It asks:

  What sonic events naturally emerge from the current computational state
  and evolution of the simulation?

Its only legitimate constraints are physical and perceptual:

- Maximum grain count
- Maximum total sonic energy (held neutral)
- Maximum CPU budget
- Human perceptual limitations

Rules must be simple and mathematically explainable — not artistic judgement
about what is musical, ambient, beautiful, chaotic, or interesting.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stability, Chaos, and Neutrality
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The engine must not impose a sonic aesthetic on the simulation.

Stable regimes should not be forced into ambient pads.
Chaotic regimes should not be forced into glitch music.

If the simulation is calm, chaotic, propagating, collapsing, or transforming,
the sonic texture should naturally reflect those behaviours because grain
rate, length, overlap, and spawn patterns emerge from coherence and change —
not because the engine chose a mood.

If the simulation is entirely chaotic and fast-changing, the audio should be
too. If it is half chaotic and half stable by area, grain-budget share and
texture should reflect that split — at neutral loudness.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Source Audio
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The user may load WAV, MP3, or AIFF.

Source audio is raw material from which grains are formed. The CA’s evolution
redistributes exploration of that material through the Sonic Laws. No
traditional effects rack is required for the listening philosophy.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Visual Engine
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Utomata remains the Cellular Automata visual engine.

It provides cell positions, RGB values, neighbour relationships, temporal
evolution, global state, editable equations, and toroidal topology.

The audio renderer observes the same state. It does not drive the CA.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
User Interaction
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Users should be able to:

- Move through CA equation variations
- Restart / reset the simulation
- Load source audio
- Observe audiovisual evolution in real time

The creative instrument is the simulation.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Sacred / Negotiable / Forbidden
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SACRED
- Audio IS the CA
- Sonic Laws above (identity, observation, region laws, freeze-at-spawn,
  geometry-at-spawn, area-weighted budget, neutral loudness)
- No aesthetic imposition (beauty / wash / chaos / interestingness)
- No structure/voice ownership or interestingness scoring
- No fixed listening points / persistent sonic locations as architecture
- Physically constrained rendering (grain count, energy, CPU, perception)
- Evolving sound inseparable from evolving simulation behaviour
- Observational regions measure the field; they do not own voices

NEGOTIABLE
- Exact numeric curves, thresholds, EMA times, grain budget N, hop ranges
- Grid size, CPU budgets, UI metering
- Debug visualisation of regions (optional; must not become lattice ears)

FORBIDDEN
- Fixed listening-point / lattice-pixel listening architectures
- Voice ownership / structure prioritisation / interestingness scores
- Using luminance→volume as the primary way to “hear the CA”
- Mid-grain sample-window scrub chase of moving regions
  (direct pan / Y-spectrum follow for region-bound grains is allowed)
- Colour → loudness, allocation, or macro schedule
- Treating the CA as a modulation source for an unrelated granular synth
- Optimising for ambient beauty, glitch excitement, or “musicality”
- Letting high activity increase loudness or steal budget beyond area share


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Success / Failure
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Success: the listener feels they are hearing the simulation’s behaviour evolve —
calm, chaos, propagation, collapse, transformation — as continuous changing
sonic texture. Large coherent areas form long overlapping textures that evolve
as successive grains follow regional motion; chaotic areas spray short grains;
overall loudness stays neutral; balance matches area weighting.

Failure: constant repeating texture; curated voices; fixed posts; aesthetic
mood engines; mid-grain glitchy scrub chasing; chaos winning the mix by firing
more; or “granular synth controlled by CA.”

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Final Creative Goal
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Create an audiovisual instrument that behaves as a miniature computational
universe.

The beauty of the visuals emerges from simple local relationships between cells.
The beauty of the sound emerges from those same relationships heard as
computational flow through time — grains as events of that flow — rendered as
faithfully as physics and perception allow under the Sonic Laws.
